# Advice: is there a genuinely different lane that abundant GPU compute opens? (2026-10-17)

Advisory note (cloud advisor), requested by the local session for the owner. Not gate evidence.
Inputs: the redesign record (M2 to T13b), `src/model/ego2s_network.py`, `train_m3c.py`
(5-step double DQN, gamma 0.99 to 0.995, one update per 128 ingested transitions), the perf
notes, `redesign_scope_2026-10-07.md` (distillation design), T13b six-snake results.

## Short answer

Yes, two lanes are genuinely different from everything the record has tried, both are
GPU-shaped, and both keep the served artifact exactly as it is: a 5 ms ego2s-bv8 network, no
veto, Mac gates. Neither will fix the late emergent deaths at L near 1,000; the record says
that is decided by search, and no bare-network lane changes it. Both can plausibly produce an
agent that is better than B8 where the products look: survival and growth in the served
rosters, Play opponents at graded strengths, and visible play (boosting, fights). I give the
first lane about even odds of a measurable product improvement and the second lane
somewhat less. Everything else GPU-heavy I can think of is either disallowed at serving,
already ruled out by the record, or a multi-week engineering bet with a poor prior.

## What "better than B8" can mean now

B8's gate advantage is mass against the served agent on scripted, frozen and mixed rosters.
The products run different rosters. At the default six snakes the all-B8 hero dies 0.392
per 1,000 decisions, 34 of its 49 deaths are self-collisions at a median length near 190,
and it boosts on 0.15% of decisions against 2% for FRP3 and 12% for the champion. The mixed
roster lifts the hero to 0.296 and median 271, and that roster is becoming the default. So
"better" today means: fewer self-collisions in crowded play, more growth in the served
roster, legible use of boost, and a ladder of opponent strengths for Play, with the Tier 1
guard on the gate mixes as the floor. All of that is short-horizon and inside the window,
which is the population T9 and T10 say is learnable (AUC 0.92 at 30 steps).

## Lane A: league self-play with population-based training

Everything B8 knows about other snakes it learned from scripted bots, two frozen champions
and a mix. It has never trained against a copy of itself or against anything that adapts
to it, and the product is self-play. A league is the standard remedy in multi-agent RL and
it has not been tried here in any form.

- **Shape.** A population of 8 to 16 ego2s-bv8 policies trained concurrently; each actor
  world seats the learner's current policy as hero and samples opponents from a pool of
  past checkpoints of every member, the frozen champions and the scripted bots, with
  prioritised fictitious self-play weighting toward opponents the hero loses to.
  Population-based training on the recipe knobs (learning rate, n-step, replay ratio, the
  boost-cost term) with periodic exploit-and-explore between members.
- **Why GPU.** Six to twelve network-driven snakes per world means one batched forward per
  step across worlds and members; on the Mac that forward competes with the learner for
  MPS. On an H100 both are nearly free and the CPU does only sim and featurisation. This is
  the configuration the T16 smoke measures.
- **What it yields.** A hero trained on the distribution it is served in; opponent models
  implicit in the weights; and, as a by-product, a ladder of checkpoints by strength that
  gives Play its difficulty tiers from one run rather than from three historical agents.
- **Risks and guards.** Non-stationarity and style collapse (everyone learns the same
  passive crawl) are the known failure modes; the boost floor and ceiling tripwires must be
  stated together, as LOCAL30 showed. Tier 1 on the gate mixes stays the binding guard so
  the league cannot buy self-play strength by losing against scripted rosters. Primary:
  hero deaths per 1,000 and median length in all-B8 Watch6 and mixed Watch6, paired against
  b8-s3. Expect 20M to 40M transitions per member; the Colab budget covers it.
- **Serving.** Unchanged. The winner is one ego2s-bv8 checkpoint; qualification as T12.

## Lane B: a large teacher with high replay ratio, distilled into the 5 ms student

The project's own best result came from distillation: M2b's raster student beat its
vector61 teacher without a veto. The pattern can be run again with a teacher that only GPU
makes affordable. The current recipe does one update per 128 transitions on a network of
about 1.5M parameters; both numbers are what MPS could carry, not what the problem wants.

- **Shape.** A teacher of 10 to 30M parameters with the same inputs plus a full-resolution
  own-body plane (the hero's own body at 1 cell per pixel; cheap to compute and the one input
  whose absence T13b's self-collisions point at), trained with a replay ratio 8 to 16 times
  higher, longer n-step, and the regularisation that makes high replay ratios work
  (periodic shrink-and-perturb, as in the BBF line of results). Then DAgger distillation into
  the unchanged ego2s-bv8 student, as M2 did: regression onto the teacher's raw Q, with the
  student acting so it sees its own states.
- **Why GPU.** Capacity and replay ratio are exactly the axes the Mac could not move; on an
  H100 a 30M-parameter learner at replay ratio 16 still runs faster than the Mac at ratio 1.
- **What it yields.** Better value estimates on the learnable population: crowding,
  self-collision geometry inside the window, boost economics. Not the chaotic late outcome.
- **Risks.** Distillation can cap the student below the teacher, though M2b went the other
  way; the own-body plane must be verified live-identical like every other input; and a
  teacher that is better only at L near 1,000 would be wasted, so screen the teacher on the
  six-snake product rosters before distilling.
- **Serving.** Unchanged, provided the student architecture is unchanged. If the own-body
  plane is kept in the student, the featuriser gains one plane and serving must be
  re-measured; it is small, but it is a change.

Lanes A and B compose: the league trains the big teacher, distillation produces the served
student, and the league's checkpoint ladder is distilled into Play tiers.

## One lane that is different but not GPU-heavy

Short-horizon expert iteration on the Mac: during training, on decisions where the top two Q
values are within a small gap, roll out each legal action for 30 to 60 steps under the
current policy in the real sim and train toward the surviving actions. This targets exactly
the self-collision population T9 and T13b identify, at the horizon where T10 says the
outcome is predictable, and it is what LOCAL30 was reaching for with offline labels before a
tripwire killed it. It is CPU-bound and belongs on the Mac as a parallel arm, not a reason to
spend credits.

## What I would not do, and why

- **Planning or search at serving**, including latent-space planners: disallowed for the
  hero, and the record shows it is the only thing that touches the late deaths; keep that
  conclusion recorded rather than reopened.
- **World models or imagination training**: the real sim is exact and cheap; a learned model
  adds error and nothing else.
- **Transformers or recurrence to fix late deaths**: T9 and T10 show the outcome is chaotic
  in the state; more context does not make a chaotic quantity predictable.
- **A GPU port of the simulator**: weeks of work and it breaks the bit-exact parity the
  evidence chain rests on; the T16 smoke will show whether CPU actors even bind under
  self-play.
- **Switching to PPO or MAPPO**: not clearly better for this horizon structure, costs the
  Q-value UI, and the blueprint already priced it as a fallback, not a lane.
- **Any further arm on the L near 1,000 deaths with a bare network.**

## Sequencing

T16 smoke first; it tells you whether Lane A is 4x on Colab. Then Lane A as the one
research line, with the own-body plane and a larger teacher as pre-registered arms inside
it rather than a separate campaign; distil to the student only once a teacher wins on the
six-snake product rosters. Keep the Mac for the short-horizon expert-iteration arm and for
every evaluation. Decide after the first league screen, not before, whether Lane B is worth
its own pre-registration. If the first screen shows nothing on the six-snake rosters, the
honest conclusion will be that B8 is near the ceiling for this game at this serving budget,
and the remaining product gains are roster, speed and the continuous-heading fork.
