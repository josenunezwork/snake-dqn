## 2. The mechanics objection is real, but “v2 was never implemented” is false

Source establishes all of the following:

## 3. How offense can win a benchmark without producing the claimed better snake

**Opponent specialization.** The scripted anchors use nearest-food pursuit with immediate-safety/flood-fill checks or seeded random-safe behavior, and their movement path disables boosting (`src/game/scripted_snake.py:10-17,204-223`). New world seeds do not make these new opponent strategies. An attacker may learn one reliably exploitable response, earn corpse mass on scripted worlds, and fail against the served FRP3+v8 opponents. Frozen learned opponents are a useful stable reference, not proof against exploitation.

**A genuine narrow win is still a win.** If the delivered hero farms the actual fixed mixed-Watch6 opponents while meeting growth, persistence, and timing bars, that is product progress. Do not reject it merely because opponents are exploitable. Limit the claim to that product scope; it is not evidence of stronger human play, arbitrary opponent robustness, or all-B8 self-play. The release roster is B8 hero versus FRP3+v8 opponents (`docs/STATUS.md:29-36`).

**Red Queen and ecological effects.** Replacing every snake changes the competition, food supply, crowding, and corpse production. Increased arena mass or self-play reward can coexist with a worse highlighted hero. T13b demonstrates why the distinction matters: all-B8 arena mass 1324.33 exceeds mixed 1222.94, while hero mass is 236.91 versus 316.76 and hero deaths/1k are 0.392 versus 0.296 (`docs/research/t13b_six_snake_roster_2026-10-17_results.md:29-36`). These are roster effects, not a clean policy A/B. Freeze opponent identities and replace only the hero for the primary claim.

**Exact simulator does not mean universal opponent oracle.** Exactness requires the actual policy, RNG and carry state, bodies, ordered food, and future actions to match. T34's shadow digest and invalidation contract explicitly includes those dependencies (`docs/research/final_run_20261019/DESIGN_V2_SHADOW.md:24-37`). M1 demonstrated zero differences on 15,000 frames, one diagnostic seed across three mixes; it did not establish arbitrary human prediction (`research/t34_shadow/RESULTS.md:17,31-51`). Branch opponents must react to each branch's altered state; replaying their baseline future actions is not a valid counterfactual. Human input, unavailable future randomness, or offline hindsight cannot be credited to a served controller.

**Proxy failure.** More credited kills can mean passive body collisions, mutual deaths, or food competitors harvesting the spoils. More food consumed can be canceled by boost burn. More short-horizon mass can buy a delayed self-enclosure. Killing weak respawning opponents repeatedly may help continuous Watch yet fail terminal-hero persistence; the reverse transfer can also fail. Measure the delivered hero's full-horizon mass and survival before crediting the proposed mechanism.

**Nontransitivity.** A beats B and B beats C need not imply A beats C. An opponent-by-opponent response matrix exposes specialization better than one Elo or pooled win rate. This is a known general multiagent evaluation issue, not measured nontransitivity in this Snake candidate: [Rowland et al., Multiagent Evaluation under Incomplete Information](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.09849). Keep the campaign's fixed equal-mix statistic as primary; robustness diagnostics do not become a new post-hoc winner selector.

## 6. Why defense is at risk of being underrated, and what would change my mind

**Survival is part of the growth objective.** In mass integral, preserving a large snake preserves its existing inventory and its remaining collection time. Illustratively, saving a length-1,000 snake for 1,000 remaining frames adds 200 H5000 mass units even with zero further growth. That is arithmetic, not a measured expected rescue effect. Calling defense merely a precondition hides this return.

**The strongest positive observation is defensive.** T3 dense changed only 75/301,589 decisions and improved s3's scripted mass by +122.812 on its reused bank. It is not “perfect defense”: six s3 episodes still died, including large-body deaths (`docs/research/t3_ceiling_test_2026-10-15.md:122-131`). Its trigger/horizon/controller and bank constrain the result. The historical census also warns against confusing length with the cause: small hazard strata were more consistent with late crowding than length alone (`docs/research/panel_mac_answers_2026-10-14.md:92-119`). Capping growth could therefore destroy value without removing the failure mode.

**Defense has real unresolved liabilities.** M2's registered recovery rules were NO-GO, and the measured serial M3 path missed all five sampled doom deadlines (`research/t34_shadow/RESULTS.md:17-27`). T3's outcome was not paced. Current T34 primarily searches on doom and preserves bare when it survives (`docs/research/final_run_20261019/DESIGN_V2_SHADOW.md:82-91`), so it can miss profitable opportunities before danger. I would not equate “offline rescue exists” with “delivery is only an engineering formality.” Nor would I infer a general defense ceiling from one failed teacher; P7 explicitly prohibits that wording (`docs/research/final_run_20261019/P7_STATISTICS.md:58-61`).

I would promote offense to the leading lane if D+O delivers a robust incremental mass gain over D, keeps survival and product persistence, retains that gain against the actual served learned opponents and withheld opponent policies, and survives the same paced budget. A credible mass-source counterfactual showing large collectable corpse gains missed by B8 would strengthen the mechanism, but cannot replace closed-loop gameplay. I would demote the current defense controller if its frozen paced test loses the offline gain or misses timing; that would be a rejection of that controller, not proof that aggression is superior.

I would stop an offense candidate that wins by extra scripted kills while losing served-roster growth/persistence, burns the food gain through boost, relies on privileged future input, or requires changing mechanics to retain its headline. I would reconsider the broader game economy only if measured provenance/counterfactual returns show that the implemented caps or collision rules make the owner's desired behavior unrewarding. That is a separate product design decision with a new B8 comparison.

The useful question is therefore: **which additional action choices increase the delivered hero's retained mass and persistence under the actual game rules?** The current evidence favors retaining the defensive delivery work and adding a bounded offense attribution test; it does not support abandoning defense on the June-agent diagnosis.
