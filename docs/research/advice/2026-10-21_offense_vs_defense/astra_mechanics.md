## 3. Quantifying offense without inventing a gameplay result

Let **N** be physical in-arena body cells at death, **L** logical victim length, **f** the fraction of retained loot the hero actually collects, **b** eligible boosted ticks, and **A** ambient pickups forgone on the detour. Ignore overlaps and later evictions only for the optimistic bounds below. Physical N can lag L; kill credit uses L while drops use body cells (`src/game/snake.py:130-132`; `src/game/game_state.py:617-628`; `src/simd_env/grid_sim.py:850-873`).

- V1 offers approximately `ceil(N/2)` unit pellets. The ambient target is a **top-up target, not an insertion cap**: a 1,000-cell corpse can add 500 pellets before overlap losses, then suppress replenishment while food is above target (`src/simd_env/batch_sim.py:934-951`; `src/simd_env/grid_sim.py:881-896`). Saying such a kill has zero value because it replaces ambient food is incorrect: location, availability time, competition and future replenishment all differ.
- V2 retains at most `min(N,150)` newly attributable corpse pellets at the default target. A 50-cell victim can leave 50; 150 can leave 150; 800 or 1,000 still leave at most 150. At N=1,000 the collectible upper bound is **15% of body cells**, versus 50% for v1 insertion, not a doubling from 50% to 100%. Existing corpse/trail pellets can be displaced and later deaths/trails can displace the new loot.
- Emission follows head-to-tail order, eviction removes oldest first. For a long, non-overlapping, unobstructed corpse exceeding 150 cells, surviving loot is therefore biased to its **tail end**, away from a cut-off at the head. This is a code-derived spatial consequence, not a measured B8 failure mechanism (`src/game/game_state.py:617-628`; `src/core/mechanics_constants.py:67-89`; `src/simd_env/grid_sim.py:435-444`).
- An optimistic same-survival length advantage is `f * retained_loot - floor((initial_boost_phase+b)/3) - A`. Killing alone adds no length. It can separately reduce competition or remove a dangerous body; those dynamic benefits require counterfactual measurement and can exceed the loot-only estimate.

**Worked example, not a result:** at N=1,000, f=0.5 and 30 boost ticks, the v2 loot contribution is at most `75−10=65` length before lost ambient intake and death risk; v1 insertion would permit `250−10=240`. A 1,000-length hero cannot double from a single default-v2 corpse's food: +150 is its optimistic +15% ceiling. A 100-length hero *could* more than double if it captures nearly all 150, so an early-game harvesting probe is more plausible than large-body trophy hunting.

**Collection time matters.** Even perfect access to 150 retained pellets requires ≥150 ticks (6.25 s at delivered 24 fps), plus travel. Boost does not raise the one-pickup/tick ceiling. On a straight contiguous v2 corpse, normal speed can net one length/tick, whereas sustained boost can net at most two-thirds after burn and may skip pellets. On alternating v1 corpse cells, ideal normal travel collects every other tick while boost can collect each tick, changing the tradeoff. These are geometric upper bounds derived from the pickup loop, not average play rates.

**Translate growth into the actual objective.** Collecting ΔL at frame t and retaining it until H contributes roughly `ΔL*(H−t)/H` to full-horizon mass, before collection time and altered trajectories. At H=5,000 and t=2,500, the example +65 contributes at most +32.5 mass; one perfectly collected +150 contributes at most +75. Conversely rescuing a length-1,000 hero at t=4,000 and holding that length for the last 1,000 frames contributes +200 for that world. This is why “survival is not a source of growth” misses the objective: it preserves a large existing mass over future scoring frames (`SUCCESS_CONTRACT.md:27-40`).

**Reward is a separate axis.** Generic reward v2 includes `0.3*victim_length`, no clamp, and −3 death, plus `gamma*Phi(next)−Phi(previous)`, Phi=L/10 (`src/core/reward_events.py:8-36`). But B8's documented recipe uses **kill scale 0**, retains that potential term, and adds `(1−gamma)*Phi(L_after)` for living heroes (`docs/research/panel_mac_answers_2026-10-14.md:18-34`; `research/redesign_m3c_20261008/train_m3c.py:307-310,1479-1483`). Thus lack of an explicit kill bonus is deliberate objective alignment, not lack of kill attribution. Turning generic kill reward back on would pay +300 reward for a 1,000-length victim even where physical loot is capped at 150; this could reward sacrificial or unharvested kills. It requires a separate learning arm, never a free mechanics fix.

## 4. Real slither.io: supported analogy and important differences

The developer's [official store description](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=air.com.hypah.io.slither&hl=en_US) confirms head-to-opponent death, edible remains and the ability of a tiny snake to cut off a large one. In a [first-person developer interview](https://www.pocketgamer.com/slither-io/interview-the-future-of-slither-io-and-tips-direct-from-the-developer/), Howse recommends following large snakes for their eventual remains, surprise boost cut-offs, and constriction; he also warns that corpse races attract competing players. This supports **harvesting as distinct from causing the kill**. It does not establish that most player growth comes from personally earned kills, or a universal doubling time.

A [direct gameplay measurement essay by Artin Ghadar](https://tomrocksmaths.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/an_approach_to_modelling_collision_risk_in_slither.io-artin-ghadar.pdf), pp. 1–6, describes self-body pass-through, border deaths without loot, a much larger player population, and size-dependent width/turning geometry. Its measured traversal times are 3.08 s normal versus 1.27 s sprinting (about **2.43×** for that observation), not our fixed 2×. This is an observational essay using a mod and limited measurements, not an official server specification. I do not adopt its quoted boost drain as a calibrated conversion to our length units.

| Dimension | Real-game comparison and evidence limit |
|---|---|
| Corpse/kill payoff | Edible remains are confirmed above. Exact retained mass fraction, pellet values, global cap and depletion rules were not verified. “Full mass converts 1:1” is unsupported. |
| Boost cost/trail | Mass expenditure is independently observed in the [CS229 real-game RL report](https://cs229.stanford.edu/proj2019aut/data/assignment_308832_raw/26588099.pdf), p. 5. A dropped-food trail is the familiar gameplay analogy used by our blueprint, but no current official server-level conversion/cap specification was recovered here; treat exact equivalence as unverified. |
| Collision | Head-to-body cut-offs transfer well conceptually. Our lethal self-body collision does not. Our 1.15× head-on winner rule should not be described as an exact real-game rule. |
| Arena/density/respawn | Real slither.io's familiar circular, camera-scrolled arena is qualitatively unlike our fixed 145×83 rectangle; exact current map size, ambient density and respawn law remain unverified. Howse's interview describes designing for 600 simultaneous players, a historical design scale rather than a current population guarantee. Our six-agent economy is very different. |
| Steering/speed | Continuous-looking curved steering and variable body width differ from our one-cell-wide, cardinal geometry. The speed measurement above is observational; screen-pixel units and scores are not interchangeable with ours. |

An honest cross-game equation is `real_loot = capture_fraction * real_drop_yield(victim_score)` versus our default-v2 `loot ≤ capture_fraction*150`. There is **no defensible numeric real/sim payout ratio** without real drop-yield measurements and a score-to-length mapping. The large-victim saturation in our v2 is nevertheless provable from source. The CS229 authors also observed useful sprinting to leave food-poor areas for richer ones (p. 5): “boost pays only for kills/corpse races” is too strong even as a real-game claim.

## 7. Cost of changing mechanics and the benchmark break

“Implement v2 first” has **zero missing implementation benefit for the default v2 product**. Re-qualifying its actual configuration/provenance is worthwhile. Making a new economy is materially different: change shared constants, both food paths and compiled Grid paths, keep bounded storage/food cost, update any value-bearing observation schema, and rerun parity and Mac product timing. The existing cap is explicitly documented as a response to food accumulation and throughput collapse; those speed numbers are comments from earlier work, not new benchmarks (`src/core/mechanics_constants.py:35-59`). Blindly deleting the cap is not a measured speed-safe fix.

V2 already permits offense to pay, so it is **not a missing prerequisite for learnability**. Existing v1 also permits contact kills and corpse harvesting. Whether B8 can learn profitable maneuvers depends on exposure, credit horizon, representation and real net payoff; nonzero physical payoff is necessary for mass-seeking offense, not sufficient for successful learning. More slither-like self-pass-through would fundamentally remove this project's self-enclosure problem; changing it would answer a different product question, not solve T34's current task.

A mechanics experiment requires the full 2×2 **policy × rules** comparison: B8 and candidate under old rules; B8 and candidate under new rules, fresh paired worlds within each rules cell. If training is claimed, replicate the recipe across five training seeds rather than treating many worlds from one fit as independent recipes. Rerun the current Mac strict and serving qualification under an explicitly new world identity; preserve historical B8 results, pins and leaderboards. Claim “candidate beats B8 under rules X,” never compare a new-rules score with a historical old-rules score. Do not silently substitute new rules into the frozen T34 study.

This read-only assessment supports a small, decision-producing offense lane alongside the existing defense campaign. It does not support replacing that campaign with a mechanics rewrite on the premise that kills, corpses or boost trails are absent.
