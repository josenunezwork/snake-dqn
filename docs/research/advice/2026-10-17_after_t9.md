# Advice: reading T9, and what to do now (2026-10-17)

Advisory note (cloud advisor), requested by the local session. Not gate evidence. Inputs:

## What T9 says

My hypothesis was that the fatal branch enters a visibly doomed pocket at the LAD. T9 says
no. Both branches sit in own-body pockets (90% own boundary) at the LAD and afterwards;
both leave the largest component; neither food-following, tail distance, local density
nor boundary ownership separates them for 50 decisions. The first consistent separator
(arrival-race area, head region) appears at offset 49-65, in 20 of the 26 pairs still
alive, with IQRs running to minus 500 cells: by then the fatal branch is in a much smaller
pocket, but that pocket did not exist at the LAD. The hero built it.

Three consequences.

1. **The late death is emergent self-sealing, not a wrong turn.** The pocket that kills
   is laid down by the hero's own path over the 50-100 decisions after the LAD. The
   LAD is the last perturbation that happened to change the path under B8's greedy
   continuation, not a recognisable decision. The Q-gap of 0.45 is correct: at that
   state the two actions really are near-equal in expectation.
2. **The lookahead's quantity is chaotic in the state.** "Does my own continuation die
   within 300 steps" is a sensitive function of the exact state through a deterministic
   policy and deterministic opponents. Simulation computes it exactly; no static
   predictor from any observation will approximate it well at a 100-step horizon. This
   means T8's raster probe is confounded: a reading near 0.77 would not show that the
   raster lacks information, only that nothing static has it at that horizon. The
   0.73-0.77 band across scalars, features and fuse may already be the horizon ceiling.
3. **There are two populations.** Ten of 46 fatal branches die within 10 decisions of the
   LAD and 15 within 25; about 20 need 50 or more. The short ones are local failures
   inside the 31 x 31 window, where perception is adequate and the surviving action is
   identifiable; the long ones are the emergent kind. T3's labels mixed both, which is
   one more reason they landed as a global prior.

So the "observation problem" framing of my last note was half right: the window is small,
but widening it would not make the long-horizon outcome predictable. What a bare network
can learn is not the outcome but a **certificate**: a property of the current state that,
if maintained every step, makes the chaotic outcome irrelevant. In snake that certificate
is tail reachability, or more loosely a floor on time-aware reachable area. T9 shows B8
has already lost it long before the LAD: the own tail is statically reachable in 9 of 46
LAD states, and in zero pairs on either branch from offset 25. Both branches are playing
without a safety certificate; one of them gets lucky.

## What I would do now

**1. Certificate audit on data that already exists (hours, CPU).** Compare the dense
ceiling trajectories (54/64 survivors, lookahead-corrected) with the B8 census trajectories
(38/64) at L >= 800: per-decision time-aware tail reachability (TTL release, as in the T9
race flood) and race-128 area. Two numbers decide the next month: the fraction of
decisions holding the certificate under each policy, and for each B8 death, how many
decisions before death the certificate was last true. If lookahead trajectories hold it
far more often and B8 loses it hundreds of decisions early, the certificate is the
learnable target. If the lookahead survivors also play mostly without it, then survival at
this length is genuinely search-dependent and step 4 applies.

**2. Horizon-stratified AUC on the T5 bank (nearly free with the T8 code).** Relabel the
same branches as death within 30, 60, 100, 200 and 300 and refit each input set. If AUC
is high at 30 and decays to about 0.77 at 300 for every input set alike, the horizon is
the ceiling and the perception reading of T5/T5b/T8 is withdrawn. If the raster or
arrival-race features hold up at long horizons where scalars decay, inputs matter after
all. This also tells T8 how to read its own result.

**3. If the certificate holds: shape on the certificate, not on death.** Training-time
potential-based shaping with Phi(s) = c * certificate(s), where the certificate is the
time-aware tail-reachable flag or min(1, race-128 area / A). It is dense, deterministic
in the current state, available every step, and not chaotic, which is everything the death
label is not. Add the same quantity as an input scalar (the T5 `tail_reachable` feature
and the region logs are close; the time-aware version is the one that matters). Disclosed
as training-time help; the served network stays bare. This is not the M6 lever family,
which shaped on length and death; it is a topology invariant. The futility bound on mass
decides whether the style costs too much growth. One five-seed screen under the T4/T6
protocol.

**4. One narrow label arm T9 does justify.** Re-cut the existing T3 bank to decisive rows
with death within 30 transitions and rerun the TWO_SIDED recipe on that subset only. Those
are the local failures where the surviving action is identifiable from the window, and
mixing them with the emergent cases is what made the margin non-specific. Cheap, because
the bank and trainer flags exist. Expected effect is modest: it addresses about a third
of late deaths.

**5. If the certificate does not hold for the lookahead either.** Then the honest statement
is that at L around 1,000 in this arena, survival is decided by search, and a bare
network's ceiling is near B8's. The choices are the owner's, not research arms: accept the
Phase R outcome under Tier 1 as the bare-network result; allow self-rollout search for
Watch mode as a policy rather than a veto; or change the game so the regime does not
arise (a length cap, a shorter horizon, or a larger arena at the same opponent count).
I would not spend further Mac-days trying to predict a chaotic outcome from a wider
window.
