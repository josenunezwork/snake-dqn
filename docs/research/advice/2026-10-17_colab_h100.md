# Advice: is Colab H100 time useful to this project, and for what (2026-10-17)

Advisory note (cloud advisor), requested by the local session for the owner. Not gate evidence.
Inputs: `research/perf_quiet_20261013/RESULTS.md` (Mac and 4090 measurements),
`t4_pod_plan_2026-10-15.md`, AGENTS.md and STATUS.md compute rules, the post-release plan
(`2026-10-17_what_next.md`, `2026-10-17_after_t13.md`), T13b.

## Short answer

Useful, but modestly, and for one class of work. The project's binding resource is not GPU
time. Training a five-seed, 8M-transition screen takes two to three Mac-hours on two MPS
lanes; the day it costs is Mac CPU evaluation, which the rules keep on the Mac. For the
current hero-only recipe, a Colab H100 would sit behind slow actors exactly as the RunPod
4090 did and fail the 4x-per-step rule. The one workload where it pays is the self-play
line already planned, because self-play moves the cost from CPU actors to batched GPU
inference. Budget accordingly: the project would use a small fraction of 1,000 units.

## What the measurements say

- **Mac**: learner-bound at 3.9k transitions/s (128 worlds x 2 actors), 5.4k with batch-scale
  2; more actors do not help. Two lanes share one MPS learner, 3.1k each when contended.
- **4090 pod**: learner ceiling 15.2k/s with compile, but actors on EPYC run 2.4x slower per
  core than the Mac, so runs were actor-bound at about 4.1k/s each. The learner idled.
- **Why an H100 changes little for the hero-only recipe**: the network is small; the 4090
  update was 8.4 ms with CUDA graphs, which is launch-overhead-bound, not compute-bound. An
  H100 buys perhaps 1.2x on the learner and nothing on the actors. Colab's runtime gives a
  handful of x86 vCPUs, not faster ones. Expect the same picture as the pods: 4k/s per run,
  two or three runs sharing the GPU, 10-12k/s total, about 2x the Mac, under the rule.
- **Why self-play is different**: with N network-driven snakes per world, every sim step
  yields N transitions and N observations for one batched forward. The CPU cost per
  world-step rises less than N-fold (the sim is length-independent; the featurizer is 23
  microseconds per agent), while the inference cost that would saturate MPS alongside the
  learner goes to a GPU with headroom. This is the configuration where transitions per
  CPU-core-second can pass 4x the Mac, and it is the configuration the plan's one research
  line needs.

## What I would use it for, in order

**0. Nothing until T15 verification finishes on the Mac.** None of this touches the Mac.

**1. A pinned Colab environment and a 20-minute throughput smoke (about 5 units).** A
notebook that clones a pinned commit, installs the pinned stack (numba 0.68.0, llvmlite
0.50.0, numpy 1.26.4, a CUDA torch), runs the grid parity and ego-live identity tests, and
starts the trainer with `--device cuda --compile-learner reduce-overhead` (Colab has a C
compiler, unlike the pod runtime image), checkpoints to Drive every 100k transitions and
resumes from them, because Colab sessions disconnect. Measure transitions/s for the hero-only
B8 recipe at 128 worlds with 2, 4 and 8 actors, and for the self-play configuration (all
snakes B8-driven) at 64 and 128 worlds. Compare with the Mac's 5.4k. Record the result as a
compute note like the perf notes; it decides everything below.

**2. If the self-play smoke clears 4x: run the self-play fine-tune screen there.** Five
seeds, the recipe from the after-T13 note (primary: hero deaths per 1,000 in all-B8 Watch6;
Tier 1 guard on the gate mixes unchanged; boost floor and ceiling stated together),
pre-registered with Colab named as the training platform and the commit, checkpoints
sha-bound and pulled to the Mac for every evaluation. Training off-Mac has precedent (the
M3-B pod runs); gates, screens' scoring and serving qualification stay on the Mac, and x86
float-tie non-identity is irrelevant to training. Cost: tens of units.

**3. The experiment class the Mac budget always forbade.** Once training is cheap, two
hypotheses become testable as ordinary pre-registered arms: longer runs (40M transitions,
where M3-B saw a peak at 10M and decline; is that the recipe or the budget?) and larger
effective batches (batch-scale 2 and 4 were measured for throughput but A/B'd only once for
quality). Five seeds at 40M on a GPU-fed self-play actor set is an afternoon, not a week.
Keep one arm at a time and keep the Mac evaluation protocol unchanged.

**4. Optional, and only if Mac evaluation becomes the queue**: development-screen evaluation
with batched GPU inference on Colab. Dev screens are not gates and do not need rowwise
identity; the batched sim runs 96 worlds per process and the forward can be one GPU call per
step. This would turn a half-day Mac evaluation into minutes, but it changes a screen
convention (rowwise CPU inference) and must be disclosed in each spec; the strict gates and
serving qualification remain Mac-only under the current rules, which I would not amend.

## What not to spend it on

- Strict gates, Phase R, serving qualification: Mac only, by rule and by the float-tie
  finding. No amendment is worth it.
- A GPU port of the simulator or featurizer: weeks of work, and it would break the
  bit-exact live-sim parity the whole evidence chain rests on.
- A bigger network: the served model must run at about 5 ms for twelve snakes on the Mac
  CPU; serving caps capacity, not the learner.
- Hyperparameter sweeps on the closed late-death question.

## Practical notes

- Pick the runtime by the work: for the small learner an A100 or even an L4 is nearly as
  fast per update as an H100 at a fraction of the unit rate; reserve the H100 for the
  self-play inference-heavy runs if the smoke shows it matters. Check the current unit rates
  and the credit expiry date (Colab units have historically expired 90 days after purchase);
  if they expire, that is the schedule, not the research plan.
- Treat Colab like a pod in governance: the RunPod rule's spirit (projected wall-clock per
  step at least 4x the Mac, measured) applies; the cost side differs because the credits
  are pre-paid, so the owner may set a lower bar for using them, but should say so once in
  STATUS.md rather than case by case.
- Expect to use well under 1,000 units for everything above. The honest reading of the
  offer is that it removes training time as a constraint for the remaining research line
  and nothing else, and that is still worth a day of setup.
