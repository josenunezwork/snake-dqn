# GPU Simulation Engine Spike & A100 Speed Sprint (2026-10-20 — 2026-10-24)

## Question & Goal

Can a GPU-resident batched snake simulator (`DeviceSim` in `research/gpu_sim_spike/`) plus custom CUDA extensions (`flood_cuda`, `event_cuda`, `move_cuda`, `vector_geometry`, `v8_indexed`) make dense-search teacher rollouts (`D300`, 6 snakes, long bodies `800–1,500`) at least **`10x` cheaper** (`<= 0.00006` Colab units/decision vs the frozen L4 CPU baseline `0.0006` u/decision) and reach **`>= 20,000` full world-steps/s** (or **`>= 2,000` complete world-steps/s** at `E=1,024` resident worlds on A100-40GB) while preserving exact feature/action parity and delivering **`>= 4x`** effective label-factory throughput over the CPU fleet?

## What Was Learned

1. **Max-capacity ring padding (`10,400` segments) and eager host loops dominate naïve GPU simulation**: Eliminating the `62,400`-iteration Python corpse loop (`A0`), hoisting `32` redundant respawn occupancy builds and `O(B)` ring scans into indexed CUDA kernels (`MOVE2` `28x`, `V8` `24x`), and bounding ring gathers to live snake lengths (`R2` `-72%` to `-75%` tick time) raised `E=1,024` A100 throughput by **over `35x` after `A0`** (`158 -> 6,010` world-steps/s on `frozen`) and **over `27,000x` vs `E1b`** (`0.217 -> 6,010–7,640` world-steps/s).
2. **CPU-exact `vector61` features on CUDA require explicit IEEE-754 RN binary64 intrinsics**: 1-ULP float32 discrepancies in danger-sector distance (col 39) and food-sector density (col 8) came from float32 intermediate arithmetic and reciprocal multiplication (`11 * float32(1/18.75)`). Using `__dadd_rn`, `__dsub_rn`, `__dmul_rn`, `__ddiv_rn`, `__dsqrt_rn`, and `__double2float_rn` with `--fmad=false --prec-div=true --prec-sqrt=true` restored `100%` bitwise feature parity with CPU `vector61_featurizer.py`.
3. **Why CPU native kernels (`S2` / `K-1`) beat GPU rollout engines for T34 label generation**: Porting the `v8` opponent veto to nogil Numba (`S2` `1.90x` whole-workload, `K-1` `20.13x` in-branch `opponent_v8`) plus `exact_stop` branch pruning (`2.9x`) raised the 12-worker Mac CPU teacher fleet to `1,989.3` effective world-steps/s (`259.6` parents/h) with exact `GridBatchSim` MT RNG and in-step food refill—leaving an A100 at `3.25x` optimistic (`1.75x` pessimistic) before accounting for RNG/food-refill porting costs.
4. **Compile-only GPU gates and bounded diagnostic scratch prevent A100 canary waste**: Mac CPU preflights cannot catch `nvcc` overload ambiguities or multi-gigabyte CUDA diagnostic allocations (`17.14 GiB` in `A1`, `9B` raw-comparator + ring-digest OOM in `T4A` stage02). Requiring a short same-pin native compile receipt and a `33.55 MiB` chunked ring digest made A100 execution deterministic.

## Why It Closed / Current Status

- **Closed on 2026-10-24** after `R2` (`PASS_SPEED` at `6,003–7,661` world-steps/s), `R6` (`DROP_E4096`), Step `-1` (`CLOSE_NO_BOUND` at `3.251x < 4.0x` vs Mac `W12`), and `T4A-DEV-01` (`PASS_T4A_DEV`) completed the chartered A100 engineering program.
- Subsequent parity verification (`research/devicesim_v2_parity/README.md`) remained archival/development-only; all campaign training and confirmation evaluation continued on the CPU/Numba `GridBatchSim` stack (`S2` + `K-1` + `exact_stop`).
