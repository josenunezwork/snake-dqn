## What T13 measured, and what it did not

T13 is clean and its reading is right as far as it goes: at twelve snakes, the all-B8 hero
dies more and grows less than the old protected hero (+0.400 deaths per 1,000, CI
[+0.232, +0.552]; median length 96 against 191), the whole arena is far healthier (0.712
against 2.109 deaths per 1,000, total mass +575), and a mixed roster lifts the hero (-0.144
deaths, +69 mass, CIs excluding zero) at some cost to arena mass.

Three things qualify it before it can drive a product decision.

1. **Twelve snakes is not the product default.** `configs/mechanics_v2.yaml` sets six.
   T13 built twelve through the T12 constructor hook and says itself that the result must
   not be equated with six-snake measurements. T12's 25 all-B8 Watch episodes, at the
   default, gave 0.424 deaths per 1,000 with median length 172 and p95 528. The same roster
   at twelve gives 0.768 and 96. The gap between the two studies is roster size, and it says
   crowding is the driver of the hero's trouble. A viewer who opens Watch sees six snakes.
2. **The old hero's advantage was the veto only it had.** In legacy Watch, slot 0 carried v8
   and eleven unwrapped copies did not: arena deaths 2.1 per 1,000, hero kills 300 in 25
   episodes, twelve a game. That hero looked dominant because it farmed unprotected copies.
   This is not an argument against the viewer's preference, which is real, but it is the
   reason the comparison "B8 hero versus FRP3 hero" is not a comparison of policies, and the
   release claim, made on gate rosters, stands untouched.
3. **The mixed roster changes two things at once** (opponent checkpoint and opponent
   wrapping) and needs code the product does not have: a Watch-opponent veto scope that the
   strict receipt never covered, roster configuration, and a qualification run. It is a
   legitimate option, not a free one.

Two product facts in T13 matter whatever the roster. B8 boosts on 0.24% of decisions
against 2.5% for FRP3 and 12.6% for the champion, so the viewer sees a snake that never
accelerates. And 59 of the all-B8 hero's 96 deaths were self-collisions at a median length
near 100 in a crowded arena. Those are short-horizon, inside-the-window failures, the
population T9 said is identifiable, not the L near 1,000 emergent kind the campaign spent
two weeks on.

## What to do, in order

**1. T13b: the same four rosters at the default six snakes (about 30 CPU-minutes, same
harness, fresh paired seeds), plus all-B8 at eight.** Report the same table. This is the
measurement of what the product shows; T13 is the measurement of its densest setting.

**2. Decide the Watch roster by this rule, fixed now.** At six snakes, if the all-B8 hero's
deaths per 1,000 are within 0.10 of the mixed roster's and its p95 length is within 100
cells of the FRP3 hero's, keep all-B8 and change nothing: a bare network throughout, which
is what the campaign set out to show. Otherwise adopt the mixed roster (b8-s3 hero, frp3-s12
+ v8 opponents) as the Watch default, after a roster qualification on the T12 protocol
(identity, veto scope limited to opponents and reported as such, fallbacks, latency with
eleven wrapped vector61 opponents) and a release erratum stating that the exhibition
opponents carry a veto and the hero does not. Twelve-snake Watch remains a viewer's choice
either way.

**3. Fix the monitoring record by erratum, not by edit.** The 0.848 trigger is a six-snake
number and the monitor already groups by driven roster. Add the T13 twelve-snake baseline
(0.768, trigger 1.536) as the Watch12 row so a viewer raising the count cannot fire a
six-snake trigger. Nothing is crossed and nothing should roll back.

**4. Point the one research line at what T13 found.** The what-next note allowed one arm,
self-play fine-tuning, after T13. T13 says what it should target: self-collision deaths at
short length in crowded self-play, and under-boosting. Both are inside the 31 x 31 window and
inside the five-step TD horizon, so the levers that failed on late deaths are not what failed
here. Pre-register it on the self-play roster at the default count with the Tier 1 guard on
the gate mixes unchanged, a primary of hero deaths per 1,000 in all-B8 Watch6, and the boost
tripwires kept as they are; LOCAL30 died on a tripwire, not on its merits, so the boost
floor and ceiling need stating together.

**5. Leave Play alone.** Opponent count is already the player's control, and the difficulty
tiers from the what-next note cover the rest.

## What not to do

Do not roll back: the release claim holds on the rosters it was made on, the trigger is not
crossed, and the all-B8 arena is healthier by every arena measure. Do not change the Watch
default from a twelve-snake measurement. Do not re-wrap the B8 hero; that would undo the
campaign for a cosmetic gain the mixed roster can deliver without touching it.
