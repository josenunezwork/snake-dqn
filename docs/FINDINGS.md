# What snake-dqn Has Learned (Findings Map)

Plain-language guide to every major experimental, engineering, operational, and process finding in this repository through 2026-11-10 PT, written so you do not have to read campaign logs.

## 0. Read this first — what actually matters (coordinator synthesis, 2026-11-10)

Written by the coordinator after reading the 54 lineage verdicts, the 72 rows below, and the full records for the B8 recipe, the PPO fine-tune, the GPU throughput work and the league collapse. Everything here is backed by a row in section 3; the judgement about what it means is mine.

### The story in five sentences

1. Between July and mid-October the project found a recipe that works: a small network that sees a heading-aligned view, rewarded only for staying alive and big, trained against a mix of opponents. That produced B8, the agent served in Play.
2. B8's remaining weakness is one thing: at length 800+ it coils into a pocket of its own body and dies. A 300-step exact lookahead fixes most of those deaths by overriding about 1 move in 2,000 to 15,000.
3. From 2026-10-15 to 2026-11-04, more than fifteen separate attempts to teach B8 that fix all failed, and so did every attempt to improve B8 by training it further.
4. Lookahead is far too slow to run live on the Mac (480 ms per move against a 41.7 ms budget), so the strong agent exists only as recordings.
5. Two things were never tried at scale: training a new network from scratch for billions of steps, and giving the network a view wide enough to see the pocket forming. That is the current work.

### What is proven and should be reused

| Rule | Evidence | Applies to game v3? |

### What fails, and the reason behind each

- **Patching a finished network.** Fine-tuning B8 with PPO for 100 million steps gained +8; with a league, −81; with a gentler schedule, −13. Fitting it to search labels taught the labels (95–99% agreement) and still lost 81–88 mass, because caution leaked into ordinary play. Reason: rescues are rare (about 0.1% of decisions) and the right and wrong moves differ by a tiny value gap (median 0.45), so any pressure strong enough to move them also moves thousands of normal decisions.
- **Predicting the trap from a 31×31 view.** Death within 30 steps is predictable (0.91), within 300 steps it is not (0.71–0.77), for every feature set tried and for a network four times larger. 87% of late pockets extend beyond the view. More capacity on the same input does not help.
- **A caution for the new work:** two attempts that did use the whole board also failed — a full-board trap detector (0.753, worse than B8's features) and a full-board value network (explained 3% of late-game variance). Both were trained on 384 worlds or fewer with no control objective, so they do not show the idea is wrong, but "give it the full board" is not yet proven either. This is the main risk in the from-scratch plan and should be measured early.
- **Targets cut off at a fixed horizon.** Regressing on 300-step returns taught the network to boost 34% of the time, because the cost of lost length fell after the cut-off.
- **Switching from imitation to RL without rescaling.** Skill was wiped out within 32–5,000 updates until the value scale was matched and a temporary imitation anchor was kept.
- **Live lookahead on the Mac CPU.** Five designs, all missed the frame budget or delivered rescues too late.
- **Shrinking the board or crowding it to make traps happen faster.** It changed the geometry of the traps.

### Where the time goes (decides every speed plan)

- In CPU self-play the network is 10–13% of the time. The flood-fill safety features are 41–57% and building the view about 30%.
- On the GPU simulator the game rules alone take 0.03 s per tick for 1,024 worlds. With the old flood features the whole tick was 0.13–0.17 s. Going from 1,024 to 4,096 worlds added only 13–21%, so the cost is per world, not per launch.
- Best measured training speed with the old features: 21,133 hero steps a second at 1,024 worlds and 28,113 at 4,096 on one A100. The learner alone managed about 140,000 rows a second for the 1.64M network, so it was never the limit.
- Consequence: the new trainer drops flood features and learns from all six snakes. Its real speed is unmeasured until the speed test runs.
- Mac serving: one CPU thread beats the Mac GPU by 3–21x for single decisions; ONNX Runtime was 2.49x faster than PyTorch.

### Colab facts that keep costing attempts

- The limit is 3 runtimes per GPU type (L4, T4, G4, A100 measured together on 2026-11-10: 12 at once), not 6 in total. A refused request leaves a record that blocks all dispatch until retired.
- L4 and A100 both have 12 virtual CPUs of equal speed, so anything CPU-bound belongs on an L4 (or a 48-CPU G4). L4 billed 1.94 units an hour on 2026-11-10; A100 has measured 5.3–6.7.
- A100 stock comes and goes within the hour. H100 is rarely available.
- Colab's control calls hang for 1–2 minutes at a time. Short caps die to this, not to our code: give every session at least 28 minutes.
- Uploads die at 300 seconds: bundle small files, compress snapshots (1.33 GB became 8.8 MB).
- Never delete or rewrite a completed export during a run; the supervisor kills the job.
- New GPU code fails on its first real GPU run in ways the Mac cannot show. Run the L4 canary before anything long, and get the independent review before dispatching, not after.
- Results differ between the Mac and Colab CPUs in about 1 game in 50 because of floating-point ties. Game v3 uses integer state for this reason.

### What the old research does not tell us about game v3

Most of October was about one failure: a long snake trapping itself. In game v3 a snake can cross its own body, so that failure does not exist. The hard problems become being encircled by others and cutting across heads, which nothing here has studied. What carries over is method (reward, opponent mix, scoring, world counts, Colab operations, where compute time goes), not the tactical findings.

## 1. The ten things we know

1. Our served bare neural network — B8 (checkpoint b8-s3, a 1.64-million-parameter convolutional network acting alone with no safety wrapper) — plays strong tactical snake (+138.67 average living body length over 5,000 frames versus the best older wrapped model), yet 93.9% of its late-game deaths still had a surviving turn within 300 steps ([row](#f-t1-escape)).
2. Giving B8 a multi-step simulator lookahead — Watch6 (6-snake spectator mode) controller exact-stop-l1-os, which checks 300-to-1,200-step survival every 5th frame and 32-step open pockets on intermediate frames — cuts deaths over 80% versus bare B8 and adds +258.69 average mass over the prior assisted bank ([row](#f-l1os-bank)).
3. Every single-state offline attempt to copy search rescues back into B8's fixed inputs failed — across 732 tested loss, head, and weight recipes, saving more than 9% of traps without changing more than 1% of normal moves achieved 0 passes because trap states look locally identical to normal coiling states ([row](#f-sandbox-b)).
4. Single-frame perception hits a hard horizon wall: static features and fresh encoders predict deaths 30 steps out at 0.912 accuracy (area under the receiver-operating curve, where 0.5 is guessing and 1.0 is perfect) but drop to 0.711–0.769 at 300 steps because 87% of late enclosures extend past the 31-by-31 local view ([row](#f-horizon-wall)).
5. Exact lookahead search is too slow to run live at 24 frames per second on a Mac CPU: across five designs, full-strength live search averaged 480 milliseconds per 41.67-millisecond frame budget (100% of frames late), which is why Watch serves pre-recorded verified games while Play runs bare B8 ([row](#f-live-l1os-timing)).
6. Putting ego-centric vision and collision-safety numbers directly into the network inputs works when trained end to end: switching from world-frame rays to heading-aligned grids plus 30 open-space and enemy-head inputs let bare B8 retire the external safety wrapper and gain +138.67 mass ([row](#f-m4-b8)).
7. Late-stage reinforcement-learning fine-tunes on a fixed small network saturate or regress: 100.7 million GPU transitions of policy-gradient training (study TRAIN-01) gained only +8.09 mass against a +40 bar, and static-league self-play (study T23) lost 80.8 mass from early food-foraging collapse ([row](#f-train01)).
8. A custom CUDA GPU simulator (DeviceSim) matches the CPU simulator with 0 state mismatches across 1,536,000 snake-ticks and reaches up to 28,113 hero training steps per second on one A100 GPU, removing the CPU simulation bottleneck for from-scratch training ([row](#f-devicesim-v2)).
9. Evaluating an agent only against copies of itself or weaker unwrapped bots creates false progress: wrapping only the hero inflated opponent deaths to 2.11 per 1,000 frames, whereas testing against diverse frozen, scripted, and mixed opponents on paired fresh worlds exposes real skill ([row](#f-red-queen)).
10. Matching hardware to the bottleneck saves 3.5x in cloud compute cost: Google Colab L4 and A100 runtimes both expose 12 virtual CPUs that are each about 6x slower than one Mac M5 Pro performance core, so A100 GPUs only pay off when simulation and training stay resident on the GPU ([row](#f-right-tool)).

## 2. Where we are now

- Watch (browser spectator mode) serves a 132-session verified replay bank played by exact-stop-l1-os (B8 assisted by 300-to-1,200-step search every 5th frame and 32-step open-pocket checks on intermediate frames, with live bare b8-s3 fallback), released 2026-11-06 PT ([STATUS.md](STATUS.md#L5-L7)).
- Play (human-versus-AI browser mode) serves live bare b8-s3 by default with an opt-in Mixed opponent field, while Mac 24 frames-per-second latency rules gate shipping releases only — large GPU teachers distilled into compact Mac students are explicitly allowed since 2026-11-10 PT ([STATUS.md](STATUS.md#L5-L15)).
- Active training work has shifted to a new main track: large-scale from-scratch self-play reinforcement learning on the exact GPU simulator across all 6 Colab slots (advisor design round opened 2026-11-10 PT), alongside an exploration run (gpu_sitl Step B1b) supervising all 24 non-value B8 layers with GPU search ([STATUS.md](STATUS.md#L7-L14)).
- Game v3 is planned alongside the from-scratch trainer to replace 4-way grid turns on a small board with angled continuous-heading movement and a larger arena map (extending Phase 6 of the July 2026 redesign blueprint; detailed game-v3 numbers are not yet committed to repo docs) ([ml_redesign_blueprint_2026-07.md](ml_redesign_blueprint_2026-07.md#L96-L97)).
- Prior results still govern both tracks: heading-aligned spatial inputs, wider arena context or memory, long-horizon mass credit without suicidal kill bonuses, diverse opponent pools, GPU-resident rollouts, and paired fresh-world gates transfer directly, while grid flood-fills and frozen-backbone patches do not ([ROADMAP.md](research/ROADMAP.md#L12-L26)).

## 4. Dead ends — do not repeat

- Offline single-state distillation or auxiliary heads on static ego2s-bv8 inputs: across 732 Sandbox B configs, C/T Stage-2, T35, and Hero Stage 1B, static 31x31 + 60-scalar snapshots alias future traps with normal coiling states ([sandbox-b.md](research/history/sandbox-b.md)).
- Live CPU lookahead search inside the 24 fps Mac game loop: five designs (Path B v2, L1', M1, H=12/H=32 Closeout, live exact-stop-l1-os at 480 ms/tick) missed 41.67 ms frame deadlines or rescue retention ([CAMPAIGN_LOG.md](research/final_run_20261019/CAMPAIGN_LOG.md#L2171-L2172)).
- Truncated-horizon return regression without a terminal body-length valuation: cutting rollouts off at step 300 taught snakes to burn 34% boost because segment loss after step 300 looked free ([m3-m7-redesign.md](research/history/m3-m7-redesign.md)).
- Pairing explicit kill bonuses with short discount factors (gamma = 0.995): caused M3-C survival to collapse to 1–11% through reckless head-to-head lunges ([m3-m7-redesign.md](research/history/m3-m7-redesign.md)).
- Narrow static-league self-play fine-tuning (T23): 8 million steps against 3 fixed checkpoints lost 80.8 mass by eroding basic food foraging within 2 million steps ([t16-t24-perf-league.md](research/history/t16-t24-perf-league.md)).
- Naive supervised-to-TD warm-starts without Q-scale calibration and temporary anchoring: wiped out behavioral-cloning competence within 32 to 5,000 updates ([solo-food-pqn-handoff.md](research/history/solo-food-pqn-handoff.md)).
- World-frame (North/East/South/West) features or training on rescaled mini-boards: mismatched relative turn actions and shifted 44 of 61 feature distributions ([ml_redesign_blueprint_2026-07.md](ml_redesign_blueprint_2026-07.md#L26-L31)).
- Synthetic arena shrinking or extra-opponent crowding to harvest late-game traps faster (DENSITY-2): all 9 densified cells violated minimum-map realism and distorted coil geometry ([bl-density.md](research/history/bl-density.md)).
- Alive-only mean-length scoring or unwrapped-opponent Watch benchmarks: rewarded early-dying boost abusers and inflated hero scores via weak opponents ([b8-release-serving.md](research/history/b8-release-serving.md)).
- Renting Colab A100/H100 GPUs for CPU-bound Python/Numba jobs unless L4 slots are full: 12 Colab vCPUs run at the same speed on L4 for ~3.5x fewer units ([t16-t24-perf-league.md](research/history/t16-t24-perf-league.md)).
- Per-file upload of dozens of tiny metadata sidecars or multi-gigabyte raw replay snapshots to Colab: triggers 300-second staging timeouts unless tar-bundled or xz-compressed ([colab-ops.md](research/history/colab-ops.md)).

## 5. Open questions nobody has tested

- How strong can a large neural network become when trained from scratch for hundreds of millions to billions of steps on the exact GPU simulator across diverse historical self-play leagues?
- Does adding recurrent memory (or sequence attention) plus coarse global arena maps break the 300-step pocket-enclosure blind spot that defeated every static 31x31 single-frame student?
