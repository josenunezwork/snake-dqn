## 2. Diagnosis

### 2.1 The "Q drift" is the v2 value structure, not over-estimation

The v2 reward is `gamma * Phi(L') - Phi(L)` (`Phi = L / 10`, `Phi(death) = 0`) − 3 on death +
0.3 × victim length per kill. Its discounted return telescopes:

`G = -Phi(L_0) + U`, `U = -3 * gamma^T_death + sum_k gamma^k * kill_k`

so the value of a state is minus its own potential plus a small, size-independent part.
`diag_q.py` evaluated checkpoints on 3,750 held-out demonstration states (rounds 10 + 12,
world seeds % 10 = 0):

| Checkpoint | mean greedy Q | U = Q + Phi | U 1%–99% | corr(Q, Phi) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| teacher (frp3-s12 Q in the shards) | +9.99 | +28.9 | 10.1 … 48.9 | −0.15 |
| M2b student (0M) | +9.97 | +28.8 | 9.8 … 48.8 | −0.16 |
| seed 0 at 2.5M / 5M / 10M / 15M / 20M | −7.4 / −13.3 / −16.3 / −17.6 / −18.8 | 11.5 / 5.6 / 2.5 / 1.3 / **0.1** | 20M: −0.3 … 0.8 | −1.00 from 5M |
| seeds 2, 3 at 20M | −18.9 / −18.6 | 0.01 / 0.27 | | −1.00 |

The teacher's Q is flat in L (≈ +10, another reward / γ); RL rewrote the value level to the v2
fixed point within ~10M transitions. The trainer's `qmax` is max\|Q\| over training batches
≈ L_max / 10 + small, i.e. the size of the biggest snakes (L ≈ 1000–1200 → 100–120). The γ
ramp barely matters for v2 values (the telescoped form is γ-independent up to U).
**Consequences:** no Q clipping / value normalization is needed for correctness; the M3 FLAG
"\|Q\| > 5 × demo max" is uninformative; M3-C logs the held-out calibration (`holdout_u_mean`,
`holdout_u_p99`, greedy boost rate) in `telemetry.jsonl` every 500k transitions.

### 2.2 Why RL adds little over distillation: the objective

With `U ≈ 0` everywhere, the v2 fixed point says: a death costs 3 (discounted), whatever the
snake's size; growth is worth nothing (the potential telescopes away); kills pay 0.3 × victim
length. The Phase R metric is the mass integral (`mass_integral` = mean over H frames of the
hero's length while alive, 0 after death; no respawn), which prices a death at frame t at
`L × (H − t) / H` and a unit of growth at `(H − t) / H`. With v8 doing the trap avoidance,
the v2 learner has nothing left to optimise except kills; the action gaps that matter are
~0.1–0.3 Q while the anchor's advantage term (weight 10, Huber β 0.1) pins the student to its
teacher: the held-out v8 agreement stays 0.80–0.85 for all 20M transitions (no departure
from the teacher), and the Phase R candidate beats the distilled control by only ~10.
Long-horizon (mi10 −18) and frozen-mix survival (−0.057) are where a size-blind death
penalty hurts most: the bigger and older the snake, the more mass a death destroys.

**Fix (arm B): the metric-aligned reward.** Each hero decision gains
`κ (1 − γ) Phi(L_after)` while the hero is alive after the step (0 on the death frame), on
top of v2, with the sim's shaping γ (so the Phi terms cancel exactly). For a snake that keeps
its size forever this adds exactly `κ Phi(L)` to the value, so with κ = 1 the value level
becomes ≈ 0 for a stable snake (the `-Phi` and `+Phi` cancel: no large recalibration from
the start), a death costs ≈ `Phi(L) + 3` (the mass it destroys, in Phi units: per step the
reward is ≈ ΔPhi − (Phi + 3)·death), and one unit of growth is worth ≈ κ · 0.1 in value. The
anchor's terms are shift-invariant, so the level change does not fight the anchor; the
advantage-Huber term does resist the Phi-scale action gaps in dangerous states (its
gradient is bounded; λ decays), which is one reason the anchor length is an arm (L).
Implemented as `--reward mass --mass-kappa 1.0` (`mass_term`, tested: the infinite-horizon
sum equals Phi).

The no-veto gain arrives late, while the anchor decays (λ 1 → 0.1 between 8M and 15M) and

reward and the length. The no-veto dev score is the primary training-progress metric
(probes at every 5M).

with-veto curve plateaus after ~10M; there is no evidence of a decline. **Evidence-backed

## 4. Boosting: screen first (B0), then at most one arm

Facts: the teacher's greedy boost rate on held-out states is 0.95% (labels with v8: 1.4%);
M3-B's students 0.32% after 20M (RL did not change it: exploration already samples boost at
~2.5% of decisions early). M2b §18.3: boost re-weighting in distillation raises the teacher's
own regret. Under v2, boosting's length cost is free at the fixed point (§2.1), so RL's
reluctance is not a pricing artefact.

**B0 (cheap, Mac, no training):** `boost_bias.py` adds β to the dueling advantage bias of the
three boost actions — every boost-vs-normal gap rises by exactly β, nothing else changes, so
the biased checkpoint is "the same student, boosting more" for every existing tool. β is
calibrated on held-out states: **β = 0.035 → greedy boost ≈ teacher's rate** (seed 0: 0.32% →
1.0% at β 0.03; seed 1: 1.2% at 0.05; the gap distribution is steep: β 0.1 → 11–12%).
Mini-screen run for this design (M3-B seeds 0, 1 final; round 52, 16 worlds per mix; paired
vs β = 0): see §4.1. **Pre-registered rule (M3-C stage 0):** a boost arm E is added only if,
on the 5 M3-B finals × 32 worlds per mix, β = 0.035 improves BOTH the no-veto and the v8
pooled means with a one-sided 90% seed-t lower bound > 0. Otherwise boosting stays a
reported diagnostic and the mass reward (which prices a boost at its real mass cost and pays
its benefits) is the deliberate boost treatment.

## 5. Layout: more seeds vs actors per learner

Measured on the Mac (MPS, 1 thread per process; `layout_mac.json`). First pass on a loaded
host with reduced replay / demo sizes and 0.3–0.5M transitions; then at production settings
(replay 500k, demo 300k, mass reward + short anchor, 1.2M transitions, after M3 Phase R had
finished):

| Layout | Processes | Loaded, short (transitions/s) | **Production, 1.2M** | Per process (prod.) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 sync trainer (M3) | 1 | 1,449 | — | — |
| 2 sync trainers (2 seeds) | 2 | 2,580 total | **2,943 total** | 1,471 |
| learner + 2 actors (1 seed) | 3 | 2,628 | **2,915** | 972 |

Component costs: MPS learner update 39 ms (host batch 2.6 ms) → one learner caps at ≈ 3.3k
transitions/s at replay ratio 4; actor-only env with MPS acting 5.1k/s per process (CPU
acting: 1.7k/s, the forward dominates). **On the Mac both layouts hit the same MPS ceiling
(≈ 2.9k/s); more sync seeds is better per process and keeps M3's exact semantics. The plan's
`mac_rate` (the G2 reference) is 2,943/s → G2 bar 11.8k/s.**

On a pod the binding resource is different. M3-B (5 sync trainers, 4090, 256 visible CPUs,
143 GB): per process 1.15k/s, env 75% of wall (0.63 ms/transition incl. CUDA acting under
5-way context switching), learner 17 ms/update; ~12.5 GB RSS each. RAM caps sync trainers at
⌊(143 − 8) / 13⌋ = 10 → ≈ 11.5k/s, below the G2 bar (4 × 2.94k = 11.8k). A learner + 2 actors uses
≈ 17 GB (rings ≈ 9 GB file-backed at 1.5× the replay share, demo 3.7 GB, processes; page cache
counts against the container's memory limit) for ≈ 2 × 1.6k ≈ 3.2k/s (learner cap ≈ 7.5k/s at
17 ms/update): **≈ 2× the throughput per GB**. Hence the pod plan uses `--actors 2`, sized at
setup: initial concurrency = min((RAM − 8) / 17 GB, guaranteed vCPU / 3.2, cap) (5 with the
requested 16 vCPU), then a guarded adaptive fill adds one run per 10 min (up to 10) only
while the GPU is < 85% busy, the **container's** memory headroom (cgroup limit − cgroup usage,
which includes the rings' page cache) ≥ 17 + 8 GB, VRAM headroom ≥ 2.5 GB, disk ≥ 12 GB, idle
CPUs exist, and the previous fill did not cut the per-run rate by > 15% (adding runs must
still add throughput). The multi-actor trainer is new code: tested (n-step builder = the sync
loop's arithmetic on random frame streams; shared / file-backed rings with overrun checks and
round-robin ingest; seqlock weights with checksums; every block — rings, weights, progress —
under `--ring-dir`, unlinked as soon as all actors have mapped it, so a crash leaks nothing;
a real 2-actor run with the mass reward, mixed worlds and file rings exits 0) and ran 500k
transitions on the Mac (policy lag ≈ 9 updates, learner wait 1%). It is not
bit-reproducible, and **its policy lag depends on the learner's speed, hence on the pod's
concurrency**: the queue is seed-major (A_s0, B_s0, D_s0, L_s0, A_s1, …) so all arms train
under the same concurrency profile, and the policy lag is logged per run.

## 7. Runner v2 (`gpu_run_v2.py`, `gpu_agent_v2.py`)

Kept from M3-B's runner (copied, tests ported): ledger reservation; balance-drop hard cap;
pod lifetime with job kill 15 min before; detached, armed-before-spend Mac watchdog (exact
pod name); delete-and-verify (`GET /pods` empty) on every exit incl. signals; the explicit
`--accept-no-self-delete` waiver (the pod key gets 403; Mac-side stop only); the 20-min
dead-man switch; definite-refusal-only create retries; opt-in stock polling (≤ 3 h) and
secure GPU fallbacks (now capped by the plan's `max_hourly` 0.95 instead of $0.80 — an owner
decision). New:

* **Plan file** (`plans/m3c_pod_plan_draft.json`, schema `m3c-gpu-plan/v1`, sha recorded):
  runs, transitions, actors, `mac_rate`, `g2_factor ≥ 4`, per-run RAM / vCPU / VRAM / disk,
  caps, speed floor, ring dir and **`max_hourly`** (≤ $1.00; used for the create, the
  dry-run and the ledger reservation; the CLI refuses `lifetime × max_hourly > spend cap`).
* **Setup speed probe:** `bench_m3c.py actor` (64 worlds, CUDA acting, 200 frames) before any
  training; refuse below `speed_floor` (1.5k/s). It cannot predict concurrent throughput;
  G2 does that.
* **Seed-major run queue**, guarded adaptive fill (§5; `fill_beyond_guaranteed_vcpu` is off
  by default → at most 16 / 3.2 = 5 concurrent runs; turning it on is an owner decision),
  **per-run stall kill** (a run with no progress for 15 min is SIGKILLed through the agent's
  new `POST /kill`, freeing its slot), and a **per-run start decision** every poll after G2:
  a queued run starts only if it alone can finish (1.1 × the current per-run time) 45 min
  before the deadline; otherwise nothing new starts ("finished partially"). Selection then
  compares arms on the seeds every arm completed (≥ 4, else nothing is selected), and a Phase
  R candidate arm needs all 5 seeds.
* **Telemetry** every minute (agent `/telemetry`: nvidia-smi util / VRAM / power, host CPU %,
  load, RAM, cgroup memory limit + usage, disk, per-job CPU % and RSS summed over each job's
  process group) + per-run recent rates, combined rate and projected finish →
  `telemetry.jsonl`; the trainer's own `telemetry.jsonl` (time split featurizer / forward /
  opponents, learner wait, policy lag, ring overruns, held-out Q calibration) is pulled every
  5 min.
* **Incremental pulls:** the trainer appends `{file, sha256}` to `manifest.jsonl` after an
  atomic checkpoint write; the runner pulls each new row, checks the sha, appends to
  `landed.jsonl`; `probe_watch.py` (Mac) starts dev probes from it (no-veto first, then v8;
  1 thread, `nice -n 10`, slot locks, failed probes retried once then skipped).
* **G2** at 30 min (the per-step 4× rule, both ways): combined rate ≥ 4 × `mac_rate` AND the
  pod's whole wall time for the step (elapsed since create, incl. upload / setup, + the
  projected rest of the queue) ≤ the Mac's time for the step / 4, AND the queue fits the
  lifetime − 45 min; else delete (Mac fallback).
* Agent `/ls/out` hashes cached by (size, mtime); the deadline download is budgeted (600 s,
  checked inside the retry loop) so it cannot run past the lifetime.

A simulated drive (fake agent, fake clock) tests the queue (seed-major, bounded
concurrency), the adaptive fill, the stall kill, the truncation, the pulls, telemetry, G2
pass / rate / wall fail, the slow-host refusal, failed runs and the waiver; a real agent
server tests auth, telemetry, the hash cache, the deadline kill and the dead-man switch.
