# Colab CLI Supervisor, Legacy Queue Worker, RunPod/H100 Operations, and Small-Bench Fast-Path

## Question and Goal

This lineage covers the remote compute, throughput-engineering, and transport-qualification infrastructure built between 2026-10-02 and 2026-10-31 to execute governed reinforcement-learning training, exact-simulator search, and confirmation evaluations outside the local Apple M5 Pro Mac. It asked whether RunPod CPU/GPU pods and later Google Colab Pro+ runtimes (L4, A100, and H100) could satisfy strict bitwise reproducibility, ≥4× wall-clock speedup requirements, deterministic resource/unit accounting, and reliable multi-runtime staging without human-interactive browser sessions or silent job loss.

## Final Verdict and Key Numbers

1. **Cross-Platform Float32 Tie Divergence Rules Out Remote Strict Gates:**
   - Across 28 H5000 episodes on RunPod AMD EPYC 7713 (`cpu3c` 2-vCPU) vs Mac M5 Pro, raw float32 Q-values differed by up to `4.8e-5` (batch-1) and `8.4e-5` (batch-256), or `2,176–3,072` ULPs (`snake-dqn-artifacts/runpod-xcheck-20261003/run-v1/comparison.json`). On 2026-10-08 (`diag-divergence`), exact float32 Q ties between `arm64` Accelerate and `x86_64` MKL/AVX2 flipped greedy argmax actions in **~1 of 50 H10000 episodes** (`1/54` in S2), superseding the strict-on-RunPod amendment (`docs/research/governance_amendment_strict_on_runpod_2026-10-05_status_2026-10-08.md`). Strict gates and serving qualification remain Mac-only.
2. **Colab GPU/CPU Throughput vs Mac M5 Pro MPS (`research/t17b_20261017/results/`, `snake-dqn-artifacts/t16-mac-baseline/`, `snake-dqn-artifacts/t16-mac-n3-baseline/`):**
   - **Mac M5 Pro MPS baselines (`w128, a2`):** `5,283 tr/s` (`20.6 upd/s`) hero-only, `5,459 tr/s` (`21.3 upd/s`) self-play, and `5,214 tr/s` (`20.4 upd/s`) N=3 static-league (`frp3-s12+v8`, `champion+v8`, `b8-s0`).
   - **T17/T17b host gather & unpack optimization (`ef32fb99`):** Row-chunked `ThreadPoolExecutor` (`--gather-threads 3/4`) cut 2-ring `B=256` packed gather on M5 Pro from `3.024 ms` (`g0`) to `1.363 ms` (`g3`) and `1.214 ms` (`g4`), and raised A100-SXM4-40GB self-play throughput from `11,341 tr/s` (`~44 upd/s`, `2.1×` Mac) to **`15,927 tr/s` (`62.2 upd/s`, `3.0×` Mac, GPU `84%`)** at `a4 raw g3` (`research/t17b_20261017/results/`). Isolated A100 learner-only benchmark (`P0`) proved fp32 `bs=2 raw g3` tops out at `78 upd/s` (`12.8 ms/upd`, `117 upd/s` in bf16), so actor-shared A100 cannot hit `84 upd/s` (`4×`) without changing the fixed batch-substep recipe.
   - **T22 + T24 N=3 static-league optimizations (`4b4723ff`, `62409ae`):** Wiring the Numba `v8` kernel into frozen pool wrappers (`research/t22_frozen_pool_20261017/`) cut Mac CPU frozen-opponent forward time from `41.109 ms` to `28.687 ms/frame` (`-30.2%`; host-only `14.458 → 2.154 ms`), while sharing retained-body release maps and learner+B8 `v8` calls (`research/t24_v8_bodymap_20261017/`) cut `L=1200` `v8` feature time from `22.155 ms` to `16.280 ms` (`-26.5%`). With T22+T24, **Colab L4 (12 vCPU)** reached **`7,625 tr/s` (`29.8 upd/s`, `1.46×` Mac N=3)** (`~3.8k tr/s` per unit-hour vs A100 `~2.1k`; A100-80GB measured `5,938–7,720 tr/s` before T22).
3. **Colab Staging `UPLOAD_CAP` Root Cause & Single-Archive Repair (`research/colab_cli/STAGING_REPAIR_20261024.md`, `research/bl_upload/README.md`):**
   - Inspection of four Oct 23–24 `UPLOAD_CAP` receipts under `snake-dqn-artifacts/big-lever/exp/` (`bl-p1/y3/source-repair-10-20261023-04/repair-output` `b675b84e...`, `bl-p1/y3/source-repair-10-20261023-05/canary-output` `573d7001...`, `gpu-move-a100-20261023-02/packet-fresh-move2-02/main-output` `d791e057...`, `gpu-v8-split-20261023-02/canary-output` `7a20b6c2...`) found that every observed staged PUT completed. In the passing P1-04 canary (`28` files, `74,138 B` in `113.760 s`), `22` sidecars (`1.6%` of bytes) consumed `80.6%` (`91.679 s`) of whole verified-transfer time. The diagnosis verdict: the four failures exhausted the shared staging clock through slow kernel/control operations and recovery, with occasional slow Contents calls; the four terminal files were not PUT failures.
   - The ranked action was to reduce package file count with a verified metadata archive: `research/metadata_archive.py` packs local files `<= 2 MiB` into one deterministic tar with an internal SHA256/size manifest (`tools/ops/METADATA_ARCHIVE_HANDOFF_20261024.md`, prepared and offline verified), alongside the separate staging control repair (`research/colab_cli/STAGING_REPAIR_20261024.md`).

## What Was Learned

- **Host CPU/RPC overhead—not GPU FLOPs—dominates mixed RL and search workloads on Colab:** In M3-C training, single-threaded Python replay gather (`obs_pack` unpack) and CPU `vector61` + `v8` frozen-opponent dispatch starved A100 GPUs until T17 (`--gather-threads 3 --no-obs-pack`), T22 (Numba `v8` in frozen pool), and T24 (shared body-release map) moved the bottleneck off the host thread. For 12-vCPU workloads, Colab L4 delivers nearly double the work per compute unit of A100 (`~3.8k` vs `~2.1k tr/s per unit-hour`), while A100/H100 runtimes serve as overflow vCPU/GPU capacity when the `L4 <= 3` sub-cap is full.
- **Colab runtime liveness and staging require kernel-aware supervision:** After Google removed the keep-alive daemon in `google-colab-cli 0.7.4` (2026-09-25), detached background processes no longer keep runtimes alive; the Mac supervisor (`research/colab_cli/supervisor.py`) must maintain periodic kernel execution, bound departed-endpoint accounting lag (2 polls), and reduce staged file count by packing small metadata/sidecar inputs into one verified archive.

## Current Status

**LIVE AND ACTIVE.** `research/colab_cli/` and `tools/ops/` (`dispatch.sh`, `ready_queue.py`, `auto_retry.py`, `calendar_lint.py`) are the active production transport and scheduling stack for the repository (`ACCOUNT_ASSIGNMENT_CAP = 6`, `ACCOUNT_L4_CAP = 3`). `research/colab_queue/` is frozen for legacy Q1 only, and `research/runpod_fanout/` is retired for T34.
