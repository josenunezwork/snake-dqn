# RL review — 2026-09-06

## Decision

**Continue the raster + batched-simulator direction, but treat the present PQN
recipe as an unvalidated experiment rather than a competitive replacement.**
The incumbent Apex champion should remain operational while that work happens;
its existence is not evidence that Ape-X is the algorithmic winner. Do not
promote a checkpoint or change the production reward or default sampler from
this review. The promotion thresholds are unchanged, although evaluator
statistics were corrected this turn.

The intended reader is deciding what to train next on this Mac and what earns a
larger run. They should know basic TD learning, confidence intervals, and
paired evaluation. The isolated `codex/pqn-coverage-20260906` worktree started
at `1b21187` (based on `origin/main` `950d583`) with a clean tree. This audit
distinguishes pre-existing assessment evidence from this turn's correctness and
observability work.

The strongest existing chassis is real: `BatchSim` has a cell-exact parity
harness ([`src/simd_env/parity.py`](../src/simd_env/parity.py)), raster inputs
are largely ego-aligned ([`src/simd_env/featurizer.py`](../src/simd_env/featurizer.py)),
and the six discrete relative actions can be masked inside a vectorized loop.
That makes fresh-rollout learning a plausible fit. It does **not** yet show
that PQN, its reward, or self-play learns the desired game.

## What existing evidence says

[`rl_assessment_2026-09-06.md`](rl_assessment_2026-09-06.md) recorded a small,
CPU-only screen and existing-checkpoint comparisons. Its available raster
checkpoints were substantially below its scripted and Apex references in short
SIMD/live screens; its reward ablation had too few independently trained seeds
to decide a reward change. Those are useful reasons to retain the incumbent,
not evidence that raster observations, PQN, or self-play have failed.

Most importantly, that screen used a different, deliberately small recipe and
does not establish a ceiling. A reported mass-integral delta is a product
metric, while the currently trained reward is a separate objective. Do not
infer a reward change from either observation.

The completed five-paired-seed, 500k-step MPS sampler campaign is documented
separately in
[`pqn_sampler_results_2026-09-06.md`](pqn_sampler_results_2026-09-06.md).
That document is the authoritative source for its result table, protocol, and

3.553, complete epoch 2.184, initial networks 2.531, scripted greedy-food

Coverage changed from 68.1% unique eligible rows for legacy to 100% for epoch,

## Current PQN implementation: why the direction is credible, and why the recipe is not yet fair

`PQNTrainer` rolls out a vectorized `E × S` world and trains only hero rows
with Q(lambda), no replay buffer, target network, or PER
([`src/training/pqn_trainer.py`](../src/training/pqn_trainer.py)). This matches
the broad premise of [Gallici et al.'s PQN paper](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/c23f3852601f6dd7f0b39223d031806f-Paper-Conference.pdf): online parallel
rollouts plus regularized Q-learning can remove the old DQN machinery. It is
support for trying the family, not Snake-specific proof.

The default sampler is not a faithful full-rollout-reuse comparison. Defaults
are `minibatches=4`, `minibatch_size=256`, and `sgd_epochs=None`
([`PQNConfig`](../src/training/pqn_trainer.py#L125)); each minibatch gets a fresh
permutation, so four 256-row draws overlap and leave most eligible rows unused
on a large rollout ([`_sgd`](../src/training/pqn_trainer.py#L635)). For example,
the blueprint-scale nominal hero set (`E=128,S=8,T=32,hero_frac=.8`, before
death) has about 27,034 rows; 1,024 draws are only 3.8% before overlap. That
confounds a conclusion about PQN with a conclusion about unusually low data
reuse.

This turn adds three **opt-in** experiment controls. `sgd_epochs=N` shuffles
every eligible hero transition once per epoch in balanced batches and emits
eligible rows, draws, unique rows, and optimizer-step telemetry.
`pad_sgd_batches=True` pads only an undersized network forward to the fixed
minibatch shape, then discards padded predictions before the real-row loss,
optimizer metrics, and sampler accounting. `sgd_seed=N` separates sampler and
flip-augmentation draws from rollout assignment/exploration draws. The tests
show the padded and unpadded paths produce the same real-row update on their
fixture and that no zombie row becomes a loss sample
([`test_pqn_trainer.py`](../tests/test_pqn_trainer.py)). The legacy sampler,
unpadded forwards, and shared RNG remain the defaults for result continuity.
The diagnostic runner [`pqn_sampler_diagnostic.py`](../src/scripts/pqn_sampler_diagnostic.py)
holds a recipe constant and marks every output `EXPERIMENTAL_NOT_PROMOTED`.
No mass-reward or default-sampler behavior changed; the separate evaluator
statistical-input correction is described below.

Revision 3 adds a separate, opt-in collapse guard after an audit found that
sampler and post-flip action entropy can censor a raw-policy collapse. It counts
raw eligible actions and stops only after three consecutive low-entropy updates
containing at least 2,048 same-dominant-action rows. This guard is independent
of sampler choice; legacy defaults remain unchanged. All ten arms completed
under this protocol before held-out evaluation. The paired screen did not
justify changing the default sampler. Revision 3 does not change the core
target or flip semantics identified below; it makes their current experiment
easier to audit.

There are further intentional deviations from the reference PQN recipe that
are not sampler evidence. The pinned author implementation applies
[LayerNorm through its convolutional and fully connected layers](https://github.com/mttga/purejaxql/blob/47af6d7b35c89ddfe633aaf7341bdb8964cb7cce/purejaxql/pqn_atari.py#L25-L67),
uses [RAdam](https://github.com/mttga/purejaxql/blob/47af6d7b35c89ddfe633aaf7341bdb8964cb7cce/purejaxql/pqn_atari.py#L177-L183),
and uses a [half-squared TD loss](https://github.com/mttga/purejaxql/blob/47af6d7b35c89ddfe633aaf7341bdb8964cb7cce/purejaxql/pqn_atari.py#L291-L305).
The local raster network applies LayerNorm after tactical, strategic, and fused
fully connected layers, but not its convolutional stacks or dueling hidden
branches; the trainer uses Adam and Huber loss
([`raster_network.py`](../src/model/raster_network.py),
[`pqn_trainer.py`](../src/training/pqn_trainer.py)). These are deliberate
blueprint/product differences and are not proof that copying the reference
optimizer, normalization placement, or loss would improve Snake. Treat each as
a future controlled ablation after target, augmentation, reward, and utilization
contracts are fixed.

Default epsilon is also a budget trap: it decays from 1.0 to .02 over 50M hero
steps ([`PQNConfig`](../src/training/pqn_trainer.py#L143)), whereas the CLI's
default total budget is 100k ([`train_pqn.py`](../src/scripts/train_pqn.py)).
A short run therefore stays near-random unless it explicitly supplies a
smaller `--eps-decay-steps`; the diagnostic does so and records it. Never
compare runs with different epsilon horizons as if their step budgets meant
the same amount of exploitation.

Self-play is a second, separate contract. Slot 0 is always hero and remaining
slots are independently hero with probability `hero_frac` when a pool exists
([`assign_policy_ids`](../src/training/pqn_selfplay.py#L100)); only hero rows
enter the loss. Frozen snapshots are added every `pool_add_interval`, but the
checkpoint does not serialize the pool or the NumPy/PyTorch RNG streams
([`checkpoint_state`](../src/training/pqn_trainer.py#L855)). `--resume` correctly
restores weights, optimizer and step/update odometers
([`train_pqn.py`](../src/scripts/train_pqn.py#L252)), so epsilon continuity is
protected; it is not a bitwise continuation of opponent exposure, resets,
actions, augmentation, or the separate sampler RNG. Describe it as
spot-preemption recovery, not exact resumption. The checkpoint now records and
resume validation compares `sgd_epochs`, `pad_sgd_batches`, and `sgd_seed`; a
checkpoint predating these fields is interpreted as the legacy defaults
([`train_pqn.py`](../src/scripts/train_pqn.py)). That prevents accidental
sampler-mode switching, but does not solve the remaining pool, global-RNG, or
reward-coefficient resume gaps.

There is a narrower resume boundary for reward tuning. The resume contract
checks observation specification, algorithm, gamma/lambda, and mechanics/reward
versions ([`resume_contract`](../src/scripts/train_pqn.py)), but it does not
include the CLI's `kill_scale` or `death_value`. A resumed run can therefore
change these target/reward-related coefficients while retaining optimizer state.
Record and reject such changes in scientific experiments, or treat them as a
new run rather than an uninterrupted comparison.

Likewise, `--seed` initializes the simulator and the trainer's NumPy RNG, but
the normal training path does not seed PyTorch before constructing the raster
network. The dedicated diagnostic does seed Python, NumPy, and Torch. Default
CLI runs are reproducible only to the extent their initial network happens to
be captured; use diagnostic-style seeding for causal comparisons.

## Correctness issues that invalidate a strong PQN claim if left unresolved

1. **A behavior mask is being used as a death oracle.** The rollout records a
   live slot with an all-false current action mask as `trapped`
   ([`pqn_trainer.py`](../src/training/pqn_trainer.py#L467)) and forces its target
   to `r + gamma * death_value` ([`pqn_trainer.py`](../src/training/pqn_trainer.py#L595)).
   An agent reproduction found a case where the fallback action survives despite
   that mask. The learner then writes a forced-death target for a successful
   escape. The source's claim that the mask is an exact fatality test should be
   narrowed: it is a safety/behavior mask until equivalence with the actual
   transition is proved.

   The smallest next experiment is a recommendation, **not an implemented
   change**: make actual `done` the sole death termination; derive a hard legal
   mask from normal actions 0–2 plus boost 3–5 only above its length threshold;
   rename the present mask to safety advice. For behavior and targets, choose a
   legal-and-advised action when one exists, otherwise a legal action. Remove
   the constant `death_value` on advisory-empty states: an actual death already
   has the stored terminal PBRS reward (`-3 - length/10`, plus applicable kill
   credit). Keep a legal-only Q-max as a separate ablation, because values for
   unseen risky legal actions can overestimate. Version these target semantics
   in checkpoints and resume validation. The present campaign keeps the legacy
   mask-target behavior, so it cannot settle PQN under this corrected contract.

2. **Flip augmentation contradicts absolute position scalars.** `flip_augment`
   mirrors rasters, swaps lateral walls, negates lateral ego values and swaps
   left/right actions ([`pqn_trainer.py`](../src/training/pqn_trainer.py#L215)).
   Yet scalars 23–24 preserve normalized world x/y
   ([`featurizer.py`](../src/simd_env/featurizer.py#L786)). A spatial mirror
   changes at least world x, so the augmented observation combines a mirrored
   world with unmirrored absolute coordinates. Remove the coordinates, transform
   them consistently, or turn augmentation off for the controlled ablation.

3. **"Markov-completing" overstates what is verified.** Hunger and boost phase
   are useful additions, but their presence does not prove a Markov observation:
   future evolution also depends on the game/world state represented only within
   finite local/coarse rasters and summary scalars. Call the observation
   a useful partial observation unless a state-aliasing audit proves sufficiency
   for the transition and reward functions. This matters because the claim is
   often used to justify target stability.

These are source findings and a targeted reproduction, not a general statement
that action masks or augmentation are invalid. Do not use their resolution to
retroactively claim that any existing checkpoint is good.

The reproduction is retained as
[`audit_reproductions.py`](../runs/pqn_coverage_20260906/audit_reproductions.py)
and its machine-readable
[`audit_reproductions.json`](../runs/pqn_coverage_20260906/audit_reproductions.json).
It branches the actual simulator state: one all-false mask admits boost actions
that remain alive, and a real mirrored observation differs at world-x scalar
23 from `flip_augment`'s result. It is an audit fixture, not a training run.

## Limits on the completed evaluation

The gate correctly uses paired seed deltas and total-horizon mass integral
([`tournament_eval.py`](../src/scripts/tournament_eval.py#L300)) and requires
two significant mixes plus no scripted-anchor regression. It has material
limits:

- “No statistically significant harm” is absence of evidence, not a
  non-inferiority result. If safety versus the scripted anchor is a release
  requirement, specify a margin and test it.
- Blueprint probes (death causes, opponent exposure, boost ROI, encirclement)
  and a current live-vs-SIMD policy/world parity suite are still not promotion
  evidence.
- The anchor environments differ between live and SIMD paths; a good SIMD score
  is not automatic live-product equivalence.

This turn adds duplicate world-seed rejection in the shared CLI parser and a
grouped-checkpoint SIMD evaluator. Grouping removes redundant full-batch
network forwards while retaining the legacy scripted-policy call order; it does
not repair an order-dependent dispatch bug. It has now been checked with real
checkpoints: hero-plus-greedy and all-checkpoint rosters produced identical
records for four seeds over 100 frames, and an all-slot 20-frame action trace
had zero mismatches. See
[`eval_engine_old_vs_current.json`](../runs/pqn_coverage_20260906/verification/eval_engine_old_vs_current.json),
[`eval_engine_old_vs_current_all_checkpoint.json`](../runs/pqn_coverage_20260906/verification/eval_engine_old_vs_current_all_checkpoint.json),
and
[`eval_engine_action_parity_20frames.json`](../runs/pqn_coverage_20260906/verification/eval_engine_action_parity_20frames.json).
This verifies the grouped evaluator against its previous implementation; it is
not live-versus-SIMD policy/world parity.

The combined-mix display was also fixed. `combined_paired_stats` now averages
each candidate-minus-baseline delta across mixes **within a world seed** before
forming a t interval, rather than treating correlated mix rows as independent
observations. Per-mix outcomes remain the promotion authority, and the
thresholds themselves did not change. This is a valid statistical-input repair,
not a promotion of any candidate.

## Resource evidence from this Mac

For this M5 raster-training recipe, use MPS with one training process and two
CPU threads. The bounded sustained run recorded 113,561 hero steps in 28.809
seconds after an approximately 8.2-second cold start; warm updates were about
.32–.35 seconds. That was roughly five times faster than the comparable CPU
updates, so MPS is the right local trainer for this specific workload. CPU
serving and the small vector/Apex paths are separate workloads and retain their
own measurements. These timings are machine- and recipe-specific observations,
not cloud-capacity estimates.

The completed final campaign collected 5,004,509 hero transitions in 36.22
minutes of training and 5.18 minutes of evaluation. Peak process RSS was 1.59
GiB and peak MPS driver allocation was 1.08 GiB; those are distinct unified-
memory counters and must not be added as physical allocations. One trainer ran
at a time with two numerical CPU threads and the campaign's RSS, MPS-driver,
system-reserve, and per-arm time caps.

The retained revisions explain why revision 3 was necessary operationally. The
original unpadded epoch arm stopped at 407,662 steps after reaching 4.318 GB
RSS. Revision 2's padded-plus-separated-RNG epoch arm reached 500,471 steps in
180.478 seconds with 1.455 GB (1.35 GiB) peak RSS; a revision-2 legacy arm
tripped at 332,548 steps in 138.306 seconds with .820 GB RSS after one 64-row
all-straight window. The .766 GB peak belongs to the first legacy arm, not the
unpadded epoch arm. This history does not show padding alone caused the memory
improvement because RNG and guard conditions also changed.

## Apex audit: retain the champion, fix its experiment accounting

Two source-static findings further argue against using Apex as the unexamined
control. First, if actors stop producing replay, `ApexLearner.train_step()` can
keep returning `waiting`; the coordinator continues its loop with a sleep and
logs an active-actor count, but has no bounded all-actor-failure exit in that
path ([`apex_train.py`](../src/scripts/apex_train.py)). Learner sampling errors
are similarly recorded and retried. This is a failure-to-surface risk found by
code inspection, **not** a reproduced runtime hang.

Second, `apex_train --total-steps` is a learner-optimizer-update budget, while
PQN's `--total-steps` counts trained hero transitions. An Apex resume also
treats that total as an absolute ceiling while restoring the learner's step
counter ([`apex_train.py`](../src/scripts/apex_train.py)); requested and
effective new-update budgets can differ. Report optimizer updates, replay rows,
actor frames, and hero transitions separately before comparing stacks. A raw
“steps” chart is not a compute- or data-matched algorithm comparison.

## Recommended method ranking

| Rank | Candidate | Evidence and decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Corrected PQN | The PQN paper supports the family under parallel fresh rollouts, and this project has its key prerequisite: a fast batched simulator. First repair target and flip behavior, clarify the reward objective, and improve utilization accounting; retain the production sampler because the completed paired screen did not justify a default change. |
| 2 | Masked shared-IPPO | [PPO](https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.06347) supports clipped multi-epoch fresh-rollout updates; [IPPO evidence](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.09533) shows independent PPO can be a strong simple multi-agent baseline. Implement only as a pre-specified fallback after corrected PQN fails stability or a healthy-data milestone. Share parameters across symmetric snakes, retain per-action masks, and compare on the identical gate. |
| 3 | Search adjunct | A shallow exact-simulator safety/lookahead control can be evaluated at serving time after a policy earns a clean gate. It is not a reason to introduce a learned world model. |

Do not prematurely port cooperative MAPPO, Rainbow/distributional DQN, or
model-based RL. MAPPO's evidence base is cooperative centralized-training
settings, which have not been formulated or measured here; Rainbow restores replay/target
complexity before its supposed advantage is isolated; learned dynamics duplicate
an exact simulator without an evidence-backed planning need. These are future
controlled baselines, not the next implementation.
