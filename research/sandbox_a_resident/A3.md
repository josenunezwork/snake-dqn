## Purpose

The A-2 A100 result established that the learner is not the limiting component:
it processed roughly 140k rows/s while E1024 collection processed roughly 2.7k
hero rows/s. Argon's A-2 review attributes the collection limit to reset-time
food replenishment, eager launch overhead, full-batch deferred-bootstrap/reset
writes, and the five-seat anchor loop. A-3 is the finite development assay for
the sandbox-local repairs to those paths. It reports timings and parity only.

## Fixed matrix and seed allocation

There are 24 cells: `E128/E1024/E2048/E4096 × ep64/ep512 ×
cached/collection/integrated`. Every cell has H128, one excluded warmup, and
three measured cycles. Cached cells report learner rows only; collection and
integrated cells report one useful hero row per world/tick.

The control uses `compile_mode=default`; each static subgraph records whether it
compiled or ran as an explicit eager fallback with its reason. CUDA-event phase
profiles record mode, milliseconds, and ms/tick for every measured collection:
observation/decision, actor sampling and rollout writes, anchors, step, bank and
reset, and post-horizon work as implemented by the runtime. Each cell records
its per-cycle profile, aggregate phase ms/tick, per-phase equivalent hero rows/s,
and the normal cell rows/s.

All A-3 reset root ranges are fresh and greater than 100,000,000. The first
allocation begins at 120,000,000, and the 24 deterministic cells are spaced by
3,000,000 indices. That exceeds `E4096 × 513 = 2,101,248`, the maximum root
range of a cell. The same-device proof range reserves 54 roots beginning at 200,000,000: 48 for resume and six for the food/mechanics probe. Packet
preflight checks full intervals, not a legacy per-allocation one-million cap,
and rejects overlaps before data.
