## Where the project stands

The campaign goal written into the standing decisions on 2026-10-08, "beat frp3-s12 + v8
without a veto using GridBatchSim, ego2s-b and distilled DQN", is met and released. That is
the headline, and it should be written down as such before anything else starts.

The late-death line is closed for bare networks. Eleven arms and diagnostics (M6, Huber,
T2b, T3, T4 two-sided and tightened, RETURN and RETURN_C, T5, T6, T11) returned null or
KILL, and T8 to T10 gave the mechanism: the fatal pocket is built by the hero's own path over
50 to 100 decisions, nothing static separates the branches early, discrimination decays with
horizon for every input, and the lookahead holds no certificate B8 lacks. Survival at L near
1,000 against scripted rosters is decided by search. A twelfth arm on the same question
would be spending against the record.

Two facts from the release week matter more than any of that for what comes next.

1. **The product roster is not the gate roster.** Watch drives twelve B8 copies and Play
   drives B8 opponents. T12's 25 all-B8 Watch episodes recorded 0.424 deaths per 1,000
   hero decisions with a median hero length of 172 and a maximum of 867. frp3-s12 + v8's own
   serving run, all-frp3 roster, recorded 0.144. These are not comparable as skill claims
   (different rosters, one wrapped), but they are what viewers see: the new hero dies about
   three times as often in its own arena as the old one did in its, and almost never reaches
   the L >= 800 regime the last two weeks studied. The research optimised a regime the
   product barely shows, and the regime the product does show has never been measured
   against the previous agent.
2. **The serving ceiling is an engineering problem, not a model problem.** The one Watch12
   overrun in T12 was 55.8 ms, of which B8's forward was 5.1 ms, the v8 flood 1.2 ms, and
   snapshot construction 39.5 ms overlapping a 38.9 ms garbage-collection pause. The speed
   ceilings the release had to adopt (12 fps at seven to twelve snakes) are set by snapshot
   allocation and GC, which have nothing to do with the policy.

## What I would do, in order

**1. Close the campaign properly (this week, no compute).** A retrospective in
`docs/research/` stating the goal, the result, the eleven closed arms and the T8 to T10
mechanism, so the late-death question is not reopened without new evidence; a standing
decision in STATUS.md to that effect. Fix the documentation that is now two releases stale:
`README.md` still calls the 61-feature Apex checkpoint the deployed reference, and
`docs/README.md` still names frp3-s12 + v8 as released. Fix CI: `lint + tests` has been red
on every push to main since 2026-09-06 (one blank line in `src/core/runtime_contract.py` for
black 26, and `requirements-dev.txt` pinning pytest below 8 while `pytest.ini` uses pytest 9
ini keys). A project whose evidence discipline is this careful should not have a red badge
on its default branch for six weeks; it is a one-file PR.

**2. Measure the product roster (T13, about two Mac-hours, during the monitoring week).**
Reuse the T12 Watch harness: 25 H5000 episodes each for all-b8-s3, all-frp3-s12 + v8 and
all-champion + v8, plus one mixed roster (b8-s3 hero, frp3-s12 + v8 opponents), with the same
seeds. Report deaths per 1,000 hero decisions, length distribution, kills made and taken,
death cause split (head-on, body, wall, self), boost rate, and food eaten per 1,000
decisions. This is descriptive, not a gate, and it answers the question the release could
not: did Watch get better or worse for the person watching, and is roster composition a
product lever worth pulling. If all-B8 play is short and brutal, a mixed roster may be the
better exhibition, and that is a configuration choice, not a research arm.

**3. Lift the serving ceiling (one to two days of engineering, then a T12B-style
re-qualification).** Profile snapshot construction, move to incremental or cached snapshot
fields, cut per-frame allocation, and tune or freeze the collector around the tick. Target:
Watch12 validated at 24 fps with zero overruns, which restores the pre-release speed slider
for the full roster. This is the most visible improvement available for the least risk, and
the protocol for qualifying it already exists.

**4. Play difficulty tiers from the registry (product, cheap).** The registry, pins and
fallback chain now hold three qualified policies. Expose champion + v8, frp3-s12 + v8 and
b8-s3 as selectable Play opponents alongside the opponent count, each already serving
qualified, with the leaderboard rows attributed by checkpoint as T12 check 6 made them. Play
against the released hero is now about 35% heavier opposition than a week ago; a human who
found frp3 hard has no easier setting.

**5. One research line, and only one, after T13: train for the roster you serve.** B8 was
trained against scripted, frozen and mixed opponents and is served against itself.
Self-play or league fine-tuning of B8 against B8 copies is the first training arm whose
target matches what the product shows, and it is the standard next algorithmic step for
multi-agent play. Pre-register it with the Tier 1 guard on the gate mixes unchanged plus a
new reported clause on the all-self roster (deaths per 1,000 and median length versus b8-s3
from T13). Expect it to need pods: self-play puts more networks on the actor side, so the
4x rule may finally be met by actor work rather than learner work; measure before spending.
Do not start it until T13 says what "better in Watch" means numerically.

**6. The product fork the blueprint deferred.** The July blueprint scoped continuous-heading
movement (smooth coiling) as Phase 6, to be decided by a memo using the v1 champion's
behaviour and human-play feedback. The v1 champion now exists and is served. Write that
memo in the next month, with T13's numbers and Play feedback as its evidence. It is the
largest remaining product decision and it should be taken deliberately rather than drift.

## What I would stop

No further late-death arms on bare networks. No search at serving unless the owner wants a
separate exhibition mode, which is a product decision I would not take from the research
side. No RunPod spend until a measured actor-side win meets the 4x rule. No new governance
amendments; the current rules produced a clean release and should be left alone.

## Why this order

The project's distinctive asset is its evidence discipline. Items 1 to 3 protect that asset
and make the release legible to a reader in a month. Item 2 re-anchors evaluation on what
the product shows, which the gate roster never was. Items 4 and 5 are the two cheapest ways
to turn the campaign's result into something a player or viewer notices. Item 6 is the one
decision that changes the shape of everything after it, and it deserves a memo rather than a
side effect. None of it touches the monitoring week's rollback triggers.
