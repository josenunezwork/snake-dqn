## E1/E1b flood verdict

- E1 (the initial GPU engine admission test) required L4 GPU cost per dense decision at most one tenth of the L4 CPU baseline and at least 99% first-action agreement with zero unexplained disagreements, plus at least 20,000 full world-steps/s on every long-body mix. (src: research/gpu_sim_spike/KILL_RULE.md:7-23)
- The L4 CPU comparator frozen on 2026-10-20 22:42 PT (from UTC) was 0.0006 u/dense decision, with a GPU CONTINUE ceiling of 0.00006 u/decision. (src: research/gpu_sim_spike/BASELINE.json:2-16)
- E1b (the revised flood implementation) replaced dense propagation with tiled connected components and sparse-frontier CUDA searches, while requiring realistic L4 accounting with real opponents and simulator events. (src: research/gpu_sim_spike/ROUND02_BRIEF.md:8-31)
- The first E1b L4 run failed mechanically because CUDA lacked the boolean `scatter_reduce` operation used by the veto painter, which the Mac CPU preflight had missed. (src: big-lever/LIVE_FACTS.md:17)
- After the dtype repair at `88adb3e2`, E1b on L4 returned NO_CONTINUE / INCOMPLETE: it completed 1 of 18 decisions within 10,820 s and measured 0.217 full world-steps/s against the 20,000 world-steps/s floor, closing that week's engine thesis without a retry. (src: docs/research/final_run_20261019/CAMPAIGN_LOG.md:103)
- The later judge traced the L4 slowdown to a corpse-candidate loop with 62,400 iterations per tick and approximately 1.2–1.9 million eager operations, identifying host dispatch as the causal candidate rather than presenting it as a measured stage timer. (src: docs/research/advice/2026-10-22_a100_program/JUDGMENT_round20.md:9-18)
- The same L4 receipt showed first-action agreement but branch mass-integral error up to −4.86, alongside counter RNG, bounded rejection sampling and cell-centre geometry differences from the CPU simulator. (src: docs/research/advice/2026-10-22_a100_program/JUDGMENT_round20.md:20-23)
